Green sukuk for renewable-energy infrastructure in Oman.

This portfolio presents my SQU research proposal on whether green sukuk can finance utility-scale renewable-energy infrastructure in Oman while preserving project bankability, ESG accountability, and Shariah-governance discipline. The thesis is finance-first: DCF, WACC, DSCR, LCOE, NPV, IRR, sensitivity testing, sukuk-versus-debt comparison, IFRS S1/S2 disclosure logic, GHG Protocol avoided-emissions discipline, and AAOIFI-aware governance.

05 — Research gap

The research gap concerns project-level finance integration in Oman.

The wording avoids weak phrases and states a definitive absence in the finance literature.

Research gap statement

No Oman-specific finance framework has yet integrated green sukuk structuring, project-level renewable-energy bankability, IFRS S1/S2-style ESG disclosure, GHG Protocol avoided-emissions discipline, and AAOIFI-aware Shariah governance into one testable capital-allocation model using DCF, WACC, DSCR, LCOE, NPV, IRR, and sensitivity analysis.

07 — Research route

A finance-led route from evidence to decision.

Qualitative validation challenges assumptions; the financial model remains the decision core.

1. Public evidence hierarchy

Use official Omani sources, NCSI indicators, MSX/FSA disclosures, CBO Islamic-banking regulation, OIA reports, Nama PWP statements, OETC material, IRENA benchmarks, IFRS/GHG/AAOIFI standards.

2. Financial model

Build a 20–25 year DCF model for a representative solar PV or wind project using CAPEX, OPEX, tariff/PPA, capacity factor, degradation, WACC, sukuk profit rate, fees, and repayment profile.

3. ESG and governance scorecard

Score only documented evidence: avoided emissions, allocation reporting, impact reporting, Shariah governance, external assurance, local employment, procurement transparency, and climate-risk resilience.

4. Scenario and validation

Run conservative/base/transition cases, tornado sensitivity, DSCR covenant stress, WACC matrix, and 12–16 expert interviews where access is granted.

Methodological justification

A descriptive green-finance essay is rejected because it cannot test bankability. A purely qualitative study is rejected because it cannot quantify WACC, DSCR, LCOE or NPV sensitivity. A pure econometric design is also insufficient because project-level sukuk structuring requires document analysis and scenario modelling under data constraints.

The interpretation layer converts sukuk documents, sustainability reports, energy assumptions, and accounting disclosures into testable variables: profit rate, tenor, reporting cost, emissions boundary, DSCR thresholds, and LCOE.

08 — Financial model

The mathematical spine of the SQU proposal.

No green or Islamic label is accepted unless it survives these financial tests.

Bankability equations

NPV = Σ[FCF_t / (1 + WACC)^t] − Initial CAPEX IRR: 0 = Σ[FCF_t / (1 + IRR)^t] − Initial CAPEX DSCR_t = CFADS_t / Debt_or_Sukuk_Service_t LCOE = discounted lifecycle costs / discounted electricity generated

Decision rule: positive NPV is insufficient if DSCR collapses under stress or if LCOE is not compatible with tariff/PPA assumptions.

Sukuk-specific adjustments

WACC_sukuk = f(profit rate, tenor, issuance fees, liquidity, reserve accounts, reporting cost, investor-base effect) Avoided CO2e_t = Renewable electricity_t × grid emission factor_t − project emissions_t

Decision rule: green sukuk are financially valuable only when all-in cost, governance credibility, and allocation reporting improve or preserve project economics.

10 — OIA alignment

The proposal serves capital-allocation discipline, not green labelling.

The practical value is to distinguish renewable projects that strengthen national asset value from projects that rely mainly on green labels.

Oman Investment Authority

Use case: evaluate whether green-sukuk-financed infrastructure improves risk-adjusted national asset value, diversification, fiscal resilience, ESG credibility, and local value creation.

Regulators and capital markets

Use case: refine sustainable finance frameworks, disclosure templates, sukuk reporting discipline, Shariah governance, and allocation/impact reporting expectations.

Project sponsors and banks

Use case: identify which variables require early negotiation—profit rate, tenor, CAPEX, tariff/PPA certainty, reserve accounts, reporting cost, assurance, and investor appetite.
